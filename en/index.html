About

About Us

To meet the growing demand for trailer houses, we were founded with the aim of advancing their adoption while ensuring safety and legal compliance.

Trailer houses hold promise for a wide range of uses — homes, lodging, even emergency temporary housing — but safety and legal compliance are essential in how they are installed and operated.

To guide and oversee trailer house installation within the prefecture properly, we have established our own installation standards and work to make them widely known. We also gather and organize relevant legislation and the latest information, presenting it clearly to users and operators so that they can proceed with confidence.

What we aim for is not mere adoption. “a new culture of living grounded in confidence, safety and correct knowledge” built up from Okinawa. Drawing on the region's rich natural setting, we will promote trailer houses that are sustainable and dependable.

What is it

What Is a Trailer House?

The Japan Conference of Building Administration defines it as “a structure that makes use of a vehicle”. Unlike an ordinary building, it presupposes that it can be moved at any time and at will.

Installation and operation must also be handled in line with that characteristic. In recent years, lodging and glamping facilities using trailer houses have spread within the prefecture, drawing attention as a new form of the tourism industry. Demand for homes, offices, shops and other uses is also rising, with adoption that takes advantage of a flexibility conventional buildings do not have.

Working from this definition of “a structure that makes use of a vehicle,” the Association makes “being movable at any time and at will” a condition of installation and promotes correct handling in accordance with legislation and administrative notices. Specifically, based on the standards set out in Ministry of Construction Notice Jushi-hatsu No. 170 (March 31, 1997), we provide guidance and information so that trailer house installation in the prefecture is carried out properly and safely.

Laws & Standards

Treatment under the Building Standards Act, and the requirements for travelling on public roads. Meeting both is the premise.

Building Standards Act

Trailer houses under the Building Standards Act

  • We follow the installation method described under “a structure that makes use of a vehicle” in the 2013 Japan Conference of Building Administration.
  • So that it does not fall under a building as defined in Article 2, item 1 of the Building Standards Act, installation shall strictly observe “movable at any time and at will”.
Road Transport Vehicle Act

To travel lawfully on public roads

  • A trailer house within the limits of Article 2 of the Safety Standards is subject to mandatory vehicle inspection under the Road Transport Vehicle Act.
  • A trailer house exceeding the limits of Article 2 of the Safety Standards requires a standards-relaxation approval and a special vehicle passage permit.

Width under 2,500 mm / Height under 3,800 mm / Overall length under 12,000 mm

The Okinawa Trailer House Association provides guidance on installation in a state that is “movable at any time and at will”.

Should we find a unit no longer kept in that state, the Association provides guidance and works with the owner toward correcting it.

Treatment under the Building Standards Act remains ambiguous in places,and views appear to differ between municipalities.

The Okinawa Trailer House Association takes Ministry of Construction Notice Jushi-hatsu No. 170 (March 31, 1997), “Treatment of Trailer Houses Under the Building Standards Act,” as its standard, and works to promote trailer houses on that basis.

Trailer houses are judged against the 13 Inspection Items as the criteria; those that can be moved at will are treated as not falling under a building as defined in Article 2, item 1 of the Building Standards Act. Installed and transported properly, a trailer house does not qualify as a building.

Checklist

Trailer house Inspection Items 13

The Association uses the following 13 items to judge whether a unit is “movable at any time and at will.” Please check them in advance when considering an installation.

1

The axles have not been removed from the vehicle

2

It holds a standards-relaxation approval or vehicle inspection allowing travel on public roads

3

There is no fixed obstruction in the direction the trailer house would travel

4

A passage from the installation site to a public road is kept available at all times

5

Steps, decks, porches and the like are independent structures

6

Multiple trailer houses are not joined together

7

Dedicated jacks and similar supports can be removed without tools

8

Water supply connection piping can be attached and detached without tools

9

Drainage connection piping can be attached and detached without tools

10

The electrical connection can be attached and detached without tools

11

Communications are used cable-free A home router and IP connectivity (internet line) are used.

12

Propane gas cylinders can be attached and detached easily with a pipe wrench

13

Outdoor units for air conditioning and the like do not impede travel of the trailer house

Standard

Okinawa Prefecture Building Standards Act Handling Criteria

The provisions on the treatment of trailer houses under General Provisions and Definitions.

Treatment of trailer houses (Handling)

Among trailer houses (vehicles without their own means of propulsion, towed to their destination by a motor vehicle or similar and used as a dwelling, office, shop or the like — that is, recognized as having an indoor use), those falling under any of the following shall be treated as buildings under Article 2, item 1 of the Act.

Further, even where a unit does not qualify as a building at the time of installation, if it later comes to fall under any of the following as a result of how it is maintained, it shall be treated as a building from that point.

  1. Those with steps, porches, verandas, fences or the like that impede movement of the trailer house.
  2. Those where the method of connecting service wiring and piping to the trailer house — for water supply and drainage, gas, electricity, telephone, heating and cooling and the like — is not detachable (a method removable without tools).
  3. Others which, judged from the scale of the trailer house (floor area, height, number of storeys, etc.), its form and its state of installation, cannot be recognized as movable at any time and at will.

A scrapped bus recognized as being used for an indoor purpose shall be deemed a building where it is fixed to the land (excluding those movable at any time and at will), or where it falls under item 2 of the main text (lifeline wiring and piping).

Here, a bus means an ordinary motor vehicle as defined in Article 3 of the Road Transport Vehicle Act with a capacity of 11 or more persons; a scrapped bus is one whose registration has been cancelled under Article 15 of that Act.

Major category: General provisions / Middle: Definition / Minor: Trailer house — Act, Article 2

Scope of “impeding movement” (Commentary)

“Impeding movement” in item 1 of the main text also includes the following.

  1. Those whose wheels have been removed, or whose wheels are fitted but are not maintained in a condition adequate for travel.
  2. Those whose superstructure is supported on the ground by something other than the wheels, where that supporting structure cannot readily be removed (for example, where removing it would require destroying part of it).
  3. Those without a passage on the site for moving the trailer house (one having a structure — gradient, width, roadbed and so on — that allows the trailer to be moved without hindrance, and continuing from the position of the trailer house to a public road).

1997 Notice No. 170
2002 Japan Conference of Building Administration, General Provisions, p.14
